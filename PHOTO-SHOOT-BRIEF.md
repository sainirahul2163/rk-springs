# Photo shoot brief — RK Springs & Wireforms

**For: Rutvik. Time needed: about 45 minutes. Equipment: a phone, a sheet of white paper, a window.**

The website currently shows rendered illustrations of each spring type. They look good, but a real
photograph of a part that RK actually made is worth more than any illustration — buyers trust it,
and Google rewards original images. These shots replace them one at a time, so you can send two
today and the rest later.

Everything below can be done on a phone. You do **not** need a photographer or a studio.

---

## 1. The setup (10 minutes, built once)

1. Put a table next to a **window** — daylight, but not direct sun. Direct sun makes hard shadows and
   blown-out highlights on polished steel.
2. Take a **sheet of white paper** (A3 or chart paper). Lay half flat on the table and curve the
   other half up against a wall or a box, so there is no visible corner line. This is a "sweep" and
   it is why product photos have a seamless background.
3. The window should be to the **side** of the part, not behind you. Side light is what makes steel
   look like steel.
4. If one side looks too dark, prop a second sheet of white paper on the dark side to bounce light
   back in. That is the entire lighting kit.

**Do not use the flash.** It flattens the part and throws a hard shadow.

---

## 2. Phone settings

- Highest resolution, standard photo mode (not portrait mode — it blurs the part's edges).
- **HDR off** if you can.
- Tap the part on screen to focus, then slide the brightness down slightly so the shiny highlights
  are not pure white.
- Hold the phone **level with the part**, slightly above it — roughly the angle in the current
  website photo.
- Shoot **landscape** (phone sideways).
- Steady the phone on a stack of books rather than holding it.

---

## 3. Shot list — products (the important ones)

One frame per spring type. Put **one part, or a small group of 2–3 identical parts**, in the middle
of the paper. Fill most of the frame. Nothing else in the picture — no hands, no tools, no tray.

| # | Part | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Torsion spring | Legs clearly visible, spread as in use |
| 2 | Double torsion spring | ✅ already have this one |
| 3 | Tension / extension spring | Show both end hooks |
| 4 | Compression spring | Side on, so you can count the coils |
| 5 | Conical spring | Side on, so the taper is obvious |
| 6 | Spiral spring | Flat on the paper, shot from directly above |
| 7 | Wire forms | A group of 3–4 different clips/hooks/pins together |

Take **4–5 shots of each** from slightly different angles. It costs nothing and gives a choice.

Pick your **cleanest, brightest parts** — any oil, fingerprints or rust will show. Wipe each part
with a dry cloth first.

---

## 5. Sending them

**Do not send by WhatsApp.** WhatsApp compresses photos heavily and throws away the detail that
makes them look professional.

Instead:
1. Upload to **Google Drive** or **Google Photos**.
2. Share the folder link.

Or, in WhatsApp, attach them as **Document** rather than Photo — that sends the original file.

## Why this matters commercially

A buyer comparing three Pune spring suppliers is looking for a reason to trust one of them. Stock
photos and generic illustrations are invisible — every competitor has them. A photograph of **your**
spring, on **your** bench, from **your** machine is the one thing a competitor cannot copy. It is
also the thing that gets the Google Business Profile ranking moving, because Google can tell an
original photo from a reused one.

Forty-five minutes of your time is the highest-return work left on this project.
